HEALPix Grids

HEALPixGridSpec provides global gridding in HEALPix NESTED order with equal solid angle per cell on the sphere. The resolution is nside = 2^level, with 12 * nside^2 cells and zero-based Int64 cell IDs. The four children of cell p at the next level have IDs 4p:4p+3. The parent ID of a child c is c >> 2. HEALPix geometry and numbering, Górski et al. (2005).

Input geodetic latitudes are used directly as spherical latitudes, as for the cubed-sphere grid, so the grids use the same latitude convention. True WGS84 cell areas vary by about 1.35% from equator to pole, from −0.45% at the equator to +0.90% at the poles relative to the spherical cell_area. Longitude is periodic. Input longitude is canonicalized in degrees before locating cells, and cell centre and vertex longitudes are in [0, 360).

Usage

using SatelliteGridding, Dates

config = load_config("examples/tropomi_sif.toml")
spec = HEALPixGridSpec(nside=64, cell_bounds=true)
times = TimeSpec(DateTime("2020-07-01"), DateTime("2020-07-16"), Day(16))
grid_l2(config, spec, times; n_oversample=8, compute_std=true,
        outfile="tropomi_healpix.nc")
julia --project=. bin/grid.jl l2 \
    --config examples/tropomi_sif.toml \
    --gridType healpix --nside 64 --nOversample 8 \
    --startDate 2020-07-01 --stopDate 2020-07-16 --dDays 16 \
    --outFile tropomi_healpix.nc

Every footprint is sampled, including those whose four corners fall in a single cell. Corners are converted to unit vectors and ordered counterclockwise in the tangent plane at their normalized vector sum. The four vectors are interpolated bilinearly at ((i - 0.5)/n, (j - 0.5)/n) and the resulting vectors are normalized to locate the subpoints on the sphere. This handles footprints crossing the dateline and those enclosing a pole.

Subpoints carry equal weight 1/n² in the bilinear unit-vector parameterization, corresponding to uniform angular sampling within the detector's instantaneous field of view (IFOV). For parallelogram footprints this gives spherical-area weighting to footprint-scale accuracy, with a small correction from radial normalization. For tapered footprints the difference follows the taper. For realistic OCO and TROPOMI pushbroom taper of up to about 0.5%, the estimated difference in cell weights is about 0.1% or less, roughly 30 times smaller than finite-sampling error in the swath-edge tests.

The gridding loop counts the k subpoints in each cell and updates its statistics once per footprint with weight k/n². Footprints entirely inside one cell contribute exactly one unit of weight. The resulting per-footprint cell overlaps also supply the optional sum_w2 and n_soundings statistics.

By default n = clamp(ceil(Int, 4 * L_max / d_cell), 4, 24) per footprint. L_max is the largest great-circle distance between any two footprint corners and d_cell = sqrt(4πR² / npix) is the mean cell spacing on a sphere of radius R. An explicit n_oversample in Julia or --nOversample on the CLI uses a fixed integer from 1 to 1024 samples per side. The upper clamp can leave interior cells unsampled. Use an explicit n_oversample for coarse sounders with L_max ≳ 75 km at nside = 2048, or for TROPOMI swath-edge footprints at nside ≥ 8192.

GPU and KernelAbstractions

Use backend=resolve_backend("cuda"), resolve_backend("metal"), or resolve_backend("cpu") in Julia, or --backend cuda, --backend metal, or --backend cpu on the CLI. All three support quadrilateral footprints, tiled and rolling time windows, and compute_std=true (--compSTD). Standard deviation rereads the files in each tile or rolling-cache day.

CUDA and KA CPU use Float64 geometry by default to match sequential cell assignment. Set geometry_precision=Float32 (--geometry-precision float32) to use single precision. Set accumulator_precision (--accumulator-precision float32 or float64) independently for weights, shifted sums, deviations, and cached daily moments. Accumulators default to Float64 on CUDA and KA CPU. Metal uses Float32 for both settings and rejects Float64. Float32 geometry quantizes the input corner vectors and orders them in the tangent plane in the same precision. It can move samples across cell edges or change the adaptive sample count near its rounding thresholds. At nside=2048, the Float32 cell-edge shift is at most about 0.33 m. For representative OCO and TROPOMI footprints, the total subpoint displacement is about 0.2 m RMS and at most about 1.1 m, below the typical 50–500 m L2 geolocation uncertainty. In sparsely grazed perimeter cells (n ≪ 1), one flipped subpoint can change occupancy or shift the mean by an amount comparable to the scene standard deviation. In the XCO₂ examples, cell means with n ≳ 2 shifted by about 0.05 ppm or less. The first nonempty batch supplies a per-variable median reference for the shifted first pass. Float32 KA runs replay the files to refine each cell's mean using deviations around it. Float64 KA runs replay when standard deviation is requested. GPU Acceleration describes precision limits, memory use, and the spherical grid locator interface.

NetCDF layout

Gridded variables, weight sums n, and optional *_std all use (cell, time) in Julia and (time, cell) in xarray or CDL. They reference the healpix grid mapping and the lat lon cell_ids coordinates. Files declare Conventions = "CF-1.13". The cell_ids variable holds the NESTED IDs with standard_name = "healpix_index", units = "1", and xdggs metadata (grid_name = "healpix", level, indexing_scheme = "nested", and ellipsoid = "sphere"). The scalar cell_area gives the common area in square metres on a sphere of radius 6,371 km. CF 1.13, Appendix F.

n is dimensionless and sums fractional footprint overlap weights, so a sounding split across cells contributes a fraction to each cell. The optional *_std is the weighted population standard deviation, sqrt(M2 / n), where M2 is the weighted sum of squared deviations from the cell mean. It has the source variable's units. A cell touched by a single footprint has zero standard deviation when the moments are finite. Spherical KA writes fill when the variance or its inputs become non-finite in accumulator precision. The calculation uses no Bessel correction. With Float32 accumulators, including on Metal, roundoff can erase small standard deviations, especially in cells with many contributions. See the GPU precision discussion.

NetCDF stores n = 0 for unobserved cells, including entirely empty time slices, and reserves -1 for unwritten weights. A cell with n > 0 can still contain science fill, for example when its standard deviation overflows.

L2 NetCDF records accumulator_precision and geometry_precision as global attributes. For gridding inputs to coarsening or regridding, use the exact-archive recipe.

With the optional sum_w2 and n_soundings fields, fractional overlaps act as reliability weights for independent soundings with a common mean and variance. Require sum_w2 > 0, n_soundings ≥ 2, and N_eff > 1, where Kish N_eff = n² / sum_w2. The unbiased sample variance is s² = M2 / (n − sum_w2/n) = std² * N_eff / (N_eff − 1) and the standard error is SE = std / √(N_eff − 1). Avoid M2 / (n − 1) and std / √n. Overlapping rolling slices reuse soundings, and soundings shared across cells induce covariance. Overlap statistics configuration.

All rolling windows with standard deviation merge centered daily moments in Float64, as do mean-only Float32 KA runs. Float64 KA restores the shared reference after merging. Float32 KA replay supplies the bounded absolute mean to the merge.

For xdggs, open the file with xarray and use xdggs.decode(ds.set_coords("cell_ids")) to decode the ID coordinate. xdggs HEALPix tutorial.

Float64 lon and lat hold cell centres. Set cell_bounds=true in Julia to also write Float64 lon_bounds and lat_bounds with Julia dimensions (nv, cell). The four corner vertices follow healpy's north, west, south, east order and are intended for plotting. Conservative regridding needs the curved HEALPix edges. Connecting the vertices by great circles changes the area of cells touching a pole by about 10%. Coordinate arrays are generated in chunks during file creation. Gridding uses dense accumulators, so memory still grows with the cell count and the number of variables.

Coarsening a NESTED archive

Use coarsen_healpix("fine.nc", "coarse.nc"; levels=2) in Julia, or pass nside=… to select a parent resolution directly. Each level halves nside and combines four consecutive NESTED cells into one parent. The output includes every time slice, the original time and window coordinates, and recomputed HEALPix coordinates and bounds (if present). The global attributes coarsen_source, coarsen_source_nside, coarsen_target_nside, and coarsen_levels record the original archive, its resolution, the output resolution, and the total levels removed across all coarsening steps.

The coarsener uses satellitegridding_role to identify weights and science fields and satellitegridding_mean and satellitegridding_std to link each mean and its deviation. These tags take precedence over legacy_std. Untagged archives without <name> and <name>_std pairs coarsen as means. When a pair exists, the default legacy_std=:error stops before creating output because <name>_std could be an independent science field.

  • Use legacy_std=:by_name for archives written with compute_std=true. Every science mean must have a matching population standard deviation on (cell, time), and the pairs must be disjoint.
  • Use legacy_std=:none to coarsen every science field as an independent mean, including fields whose names end in _std.

For each parent, the coarsener sums child weights and combines child means and central moments with Chan's formula in Float64. It recovers each child's central moment as M2 = n * std^2 and writes the parent's weighted population standard deviation as sqrt(M2 / n). It skips cells with n = 0. A missing mean in a positive-weight child propagates to the parent mean and standard deviation for that variable. A missing child standard deviation propagates to the parent standard deviation.

Exact-archive recipe

Grid the fine archive with Float64 accumulation and spherical geometry. In Julia, use HEALPixGridSpec(; nside=..., T=Float64) for sequential gridding. On the sequential CLI, use --accumulator-precision float64. KA CPU and CUDA default to Float64 for both precisions. Enable standard deviations with compute_std=true in Julia or --compSTD on the CLI.

[output]
precision = "float64"
significant_digits_mean = "none"
significant_digits_std = "none"
fill_value = nan

Coarsening preserves the moments of the archived sub-sample set up to Float64 summation and serialization roundoff, including with adaptive oversampling. To reproduce those moments by gridding directly at the parent resolution, use the same fixed n_oversample (--nOversample on the CLI), geometry precision, and time windows for the two runs. Adaptive oversampling can choose different sub-samples at each resolution. Quantization of nearly equal serialized child means can dominate a small parent standard deviation. Coarsening writes Float64 without decimal rounding and warns about Float32 accumulation, Float32 storage, decimal rounding, and missing precision or rounding metadata.

Coarsened overlap statistics

When present, sum_w2 and n_soundings are summed across descendants and marked with coarsening_approximation. A missing statistic in any observed child propagates to that parent statistic. For L total levels removed, compare with parent statistics for the same archived sub-samples. The summed sum_w2 is between 4⁻ᴸ and 1 times the true parent value, while the summed n_soundings is between 1 and 4ᴸ times the true parent count. A footprint touching multiple descendants contributes multiple counts and loses the cross-products of its weights. This can inflate N_eff by up to 4ᴸ. Do not use these sums for sample variance or SE. Even one footprint can pass both estimator guards after coarsening and give a spurious zero SE. Grid directly at the parent resolution for these estimates.

In a one-day OCO-2 run on CUDA with Float64 and no decimal rounding, using n_oversample=4, coarsening from nside=512 to 128 matched direct nside=128 gridding with zero difference in n, a maximum mean difference of 6 × 10⁻¹⁴ ppm, and a maximum relative standard deviation difference of 9 × 10⁻¹⁴. The summed n_soundings was 20% higher (97,157 vs 81,077) and the summed sum_w2 was 6.6% lower (69,099 vs 73,950).

Known limitations and planned work

HEALPix supports quadrilateral Level-2 footprints to NetCDF. Circular footprints, centre-coordinate gridding such as MODIS, Zarr output, and UGRID export are outside this support.

  • Per-cell WGS84 ellipsoidal areas are planned separately.
